`ImplicitSurface<T, F>` with the function as a type, `make_implicit<T>` from a generic lambda: the gradient exact through a `Dual`, the Hessian exact through a `Dual` of a `Dual`, the Gaussian curvature that follows from the two -- K = grad F . adj(H) grad F / |grad F|^4 -- and exp and log as geodesics in the ambient space: a curve on F = 0 has grad F . x' = 0, differentiating again grad F . x'' = -x'^T H x', and a geodesic's acceleration is normal to the surface, so x'' = -(x'^T H x') / |grad F|^2 grad F, integrated by the extrapolated flow and put back on the surface. Regular wherever grad F is: no chart, so no pole. The erased form keeps its first-order retraction and its difference-quotient gradient. Three routes that share nothing but the surface meet in the tests and agree: the unit sphere as a level set against the closed forms of `Sphere<2>` (exp, distance, exp after log, 1e-8); a torus as a level set against the same torus as a chart, whose geodesics come from the induced metric's Christoffel symbols (exp at three times, distance and log, 1e-8); and Gauss-Bonnet, the first time the library has had it, through two descriptions that neither knows the answer to -- K from the implicit function, dA from the chart's area element, integrated by `integrate_with_error` (status read: Converged) -- 4 pi on the sphere and 0 on the torus. The matrix gains the typed level-set sphere beside the typed torus -- and, over `Dual`, a hole in `Dual` itself: `Dual<Dual<Dual<double>>> - Dual<double>` did not compile, because a constant of a lower depth on the right needs two converting constructors in a row (on the left it worked), so the operators gain their right-hand forms; the typed torus had its constants on the left and never met it. The Fermat family -- sum x_i^n = 1 as a typed `ImplicitSurface` -- is now one lambda and the same two checks.
