title: The Fermat family, and Gauss-Bonnet by two routes
---
The idea that began with a question about Fermat's Last Theorem -- a one-parameter family whose invariants stay while the shape does not -- is a test: sum x_i^n = 1 for even n, Lame's superellipsoids, as typed `ImplicitSurface`s from one lambda. n = 2 is the unit sphere (K = 1, the geodesics of `Sphere<2>`); as n grows the surface flattens toward the cube and the curvature gathers at its eight corners (K at the corner rises with n, K on a face falls); the diagonal point 3^(-1/n) goes to 1. Through all of it the surface is a sphere, so the total curvature is 4 pi and a mesh has Euler characteristic 2. Two routes to 4 pi that share nothing: the smooth path, K from the implicit function through nested Duals and dA from a radial chart of the same surface, by `integrate_with_error`; and the mesh path, the sum of the angle defects 2 pi - (angles at a vertex) over a marching-cubes mesh, which is 2 pi chi for any closed triangle mesh -- a combinatorial identity with no curvature in it. The mesh gives 4 pi to 1e-9 at n = 2, 4, 6, 8, chi = 2 each time. The smooth path gives it to 6e-8 at n = 2 and 4, to 1e-5 at n = 6 -- and at n = 8 to 1e-3, with the rule reporting an estimate of 3e-9 and the status Suspicious: the integrand is eight sharp peaks, one rule's estimate is small and wrong, and it is the other witness -- the mesh, or the known 4 pi -- that shows it. The status was the rule's own warning, and it was given. What the family does not yet do: n -> infinity against `Box`'s distance and CCD, and the pair by `exp`/`log` at n > 2 (no closed form to compare with; the level set against a chart of the same surface is the pair, as for the torus).
