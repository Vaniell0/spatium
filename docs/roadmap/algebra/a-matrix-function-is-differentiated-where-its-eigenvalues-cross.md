Four cells of the matrix were at L0: the derivative of the affine-invariant SPD distance through `Dual`, on `SPDAff` and on `SPDAffxS2`, for `Dual` and `Dual2`. The value was finite and the derivative was NaN. The cause is that `exp_map(p, v, t)` at t = 0 hands `exp` the matrix t P^-1/2 V P^-1/2, which is zero, and the eigenvalues of zero coincide: the closed-form eigenvalues of a 2 by 2 matrix are mean plus or minus sqrt(disc), the discriminant is t^2 times something, and the chain rule through the square root of a value that is zero is infinity times zero. A function of a symmetric matrix is analytic where its eigenvalues cross; it is the eigendecomposition that is not, and the derivative does not need to go through it. `apply_eigen_sym` on a matrix of `Dual`s now takes the decomposition of the values, calls the function once per eigenvalue on a `Dual` with slope 1 (which gives f and f' together), and differentiates by the Daleckii-Krein formula d f(S) = V (Gamma o V^T dS V) V^T, with the divided difference of f where the eigenvalues differ and f' where they coincide. For `Dual2` the same code runs on the inner `Dual`. The four cells are L3 now, and no other cell of the matrix moved (regenerated: `docs/connectivity.md` and `expected.hpp` differ in exactly those).

A first try was wrong and is worth the record. Making the chain rule of `Dual` keep a derivative of 0 at 0 (so that a constant stays a constant through `sqrt`) removed the NaN and left a derivative that was finite and wrong, 0.57 against a difference quotient of 0.64, because zero first derivative is not constancy. It was reverted; the NaN was telling the truth.

The comparison against a difference quotient found a second thing, nothing to do with `Dual`. `eigen_sym` for 2 by 2 took the eigenvalues from the characteristic quadratic and the vectors as (b, lambda - a), and V diag(sqrt lambda) V^T missed the matrix by 1e-3 at a distance of 1e-6 from the identity and by 0.15 at 1e-7: the error goes as eps / gap^2, because lambda - a is a difference of nearly equal numbers. It uses the half-angle formulas now (c = (a - d)/2 scaled by the larger of |c| and |b|, r = hypot, the vector of the larger eigenvalue from whichever of (c + r, b) and (b, r - c) subtracts nothing like), orthonormal by construction; the reconstruction error is 1e-16 at every distance. The scaling is not decoration: without it a float matrix within 1e-23 of a scalar one underflowed `c*c + b*b` and the normalisation was 0/0, which turned the Frechet mean of SPD x S2 in float to NaN. A pair in the registry holds it (`spd2_near_identity`: sqrt, inverse sqrt and log of c I + hM for h between 1e-9 and 1e-1, double against `Real50`); on the previous solver 3665 of 4000 inputs fail, by up to 1e13 times the bound.
