`algebra/octonion.hpp`: the normed division algebra of dimension 8 by Cayley-Dickson doubling of the library's own `Quaternion` -- (a, b)(c, d) = (ac - d*b, da + bc*) -- not a table of structure constants. There is no second copy of the product to compare it with and none is needed: a unital composition algebra of dimension 8 is the octonions (Hurwitz), so a wrong sign breaks one of the properties the tests hold -- `|ab| = |a||b|` on 500 random pairs, alternativity, the Moufang identities, non-associativity with a witness ((e1 e2) e4 = -e1 (e2 e4)) and on random triples, a two-sided inverse, and the embeddings of `Quaternion` and `Complex` -- over `double`, over `Real50` at 1e-45, and over `Dual`, where the derivative of |ab| through the product and through |a||b| must agree. From it `cross7(x, y)`, the imaginary part of the product of two imaginary octonions: orthogonal to both factors, `|x cross y|^2 = |x|^2|y|^2 - (x.y)^2`, antisymmetric, and without the Jacobi identity R^3's has (it fails on 190 of 200 random triples). And `Sphere<N>::tangent_frame(p)`: the point times each imaginary unit, N orthonormal tangent vectors depending polynomially on p, a member that exists at N = 1, 3 and 7 and at no other -- complex numbers, quaternions, octonions -- so asking `Sphere<2>` for one does not compile, which is the hairy ball theorem rather than a gap. `tests/test_dimension_theorems.cpp` now says the three rows are closed: the cross product is implemented at {3, 7}, the division algebras at {1, 2, 4, 8}, the frames at {1, 3, 7}, each equal to the set the theorem allows. What is left allowed and unwritten: the group structure on S^1 and S^3.
