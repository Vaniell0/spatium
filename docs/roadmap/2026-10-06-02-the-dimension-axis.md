Some structures exist at certain dimensions only, by theorem -- a cross product at 3 and 7, a normed division algebra at 1, 2, 4 and 8, a group structure on S^1 and S^3, a global tangent frame on S^1, S^3 and S^7 (`docs/dimensions.md`) -- and code written for one N may quietly assume it. The three families now run through every probe at N = 1, 3, 4, 7 and 8 besides the three the matrix always had, on `double`, `Real50` and `Dual` (a dimension belongs to the space, not the scalar): 1104 cells, 1051 at L2 or above, all ten new spaces L3. They were uniform; the exception was the one the axis was for. The Frechet mean on `Hyperbolic<8>` returned NaN: its Karcher step of 1 is exact in a flat space and right near the mean on a sphere, but overshoots in negative curvature once d coth d passes 2, a spread of about 1.5, and random tangents are longer in eight dimensions than in two. `frechet_mean` now halves the step until the variance falls, with the rounding of the variance as slack so that near the solution the first step is always taken. The other finding was the probe's: `MetricAxioms` summed distances after rounding them to double, an error of 2e-16 against a `Real50` tolerance of 4e-23, which three collinear points at N = 1 -- the inequality with equality -- exposed; it now compares in the cell's own arithmetic. `tests/test_dimension_theorems.cpp` holds the library to the direction a machine can check: what it implements lies inside what the theorem allows (a `cross` at N = 4, or a group structure on S^2, would fail it); allowed and not implemented (the 7-dimensional cross product, the octonions, the groups S^1 and S^3) is a gap of glue and listed, not a failure. A red cell of the matrix is now of three kinds: no glue, no support, impossible by theorem.
