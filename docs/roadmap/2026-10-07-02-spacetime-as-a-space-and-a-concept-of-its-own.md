`spaces/minkowski.hpp`: `Minkowski<N>` in geometric units, c = 1, events (t, x_1..x_N), signature (-, +, ..., +) -- the form `Hyperbolic` is written on. c is not a parameter of the type and not a field, so nothing flows through the operations (`Kerr` and `Schwarzschild` are written the same way); where a dimensional quantity is wanted c is an ordinary scalar argument of an ordinary function, and a limit in c is a limit of that function. A Lorentzian space is a different concept from a Riemannian one, not a weaker one: `LorentzianManifold` (core/concepts.hpp) asks for `interval`, `causal` (`Causal`: timelike, null, spacelike), `proper_time` and `precedes`, and a space that satisfies it is deliberately not a `MetricSpace` -- there is no distance (zero between distinct points on a light cone, the triangle inequality reversed for events a signal can join). It is therefore not a red cell of the connectivity matrix, which grades the Riemannian concept; the difference is recorded in `docs/architecture.md`'s concept tree, and what replaces the metric axioms is `verify_lorentzian`: the interval symmetric and zero on the diagonal, the causal class agreeing with its sign, and the reverse triangle inequality (the straight clock reads longest). It has its control: a space with two time directions, signature (-, -, +), fails the reverse triangle inequality (a detour reads 2.12 against 1.80 straight). `boost(v)` writes gamma^2/(gamma + 1) for (gamma - 1)/v^2, so the zero boost is the identity with no 0/0. The velocities of special relativity are a hyperbolic space: the four-velocities gamma (1, v) are points of `Hyperbolic<N>`, and a rapidity is a hyperbolic distance (atanh of the speed). Pairs that share nothing: collinear velocities add by the relativistic formula, by the sum of rapidities and by the composition of boost matrices, to 1e-13; the Thomas-Wigner rotation of two perpendicular boosts -- read off the composition of the matrices after the composite boost is stripped -- is the angular defect of the triangle (rest, u, composite) in `Hyperbolic<2>` and equals the closed form cos = (g_u + g_v)/(1 + g_u g_v), to 1e-10 at (0.6, 0.5), (0.9, 0.8) and (0.3, 0.2). And the first use of `Series` for physics: `kinetic_energy(m, v, c)` over a `Series` with c = 1/eps gives 1/2 m v^2 + 3/8 m v^4/c^2 + 5/16 m v^6/c^4 exactly (to 1e-14, only even powers of 1/c), where a double at c = 1e8 is a difference of two equal numbers and is off by more than 10%. Not done: the family as a row of the connectivity matrix, the Schwarzschild-to-Minkowski limit at infinity, the Kretschmann scalar read as 48M^2/r^6.
