Stage 2.6. A dimension was a `std::size_t` read against two sentinels (`kDynamic`, and nothing at all for a space of functions). `core/dimension.hpp`: `Dimension` is a literal value of one of three kinds -- finite (N coordinates, stored in a `Vec` or a matrix), dynamic (known at run time) and infinite (no finite basis) -- with `is_finite()`, `is_dynamic()`, `is_infinite()`, and sums that mean what they say: an infinite factor makes a product infinite, a dynamic one makes it dynamic, finite ones add (a plain `size_t` sum would have overflowed on the sentinel). A `std::size_t` still converts both ways and a `Dimension` reads back as the count or the sentinel, so every `static constexpr std::size_t dimension = N;` written before is a dimension unchanged: the whole project, 36 declarations and the arithmetic on them in `mesh/operations.hpp` and `physics/mechanics/body.hpp`, built without a line changed in them, and the 1256 tests pass. What changed is where the kind is read: `EuclideanSpace` asks for a finite dimension (it said `!= kDynamic`, which an infinite one would have passed), and `ProductSpace::dimension` is a `Dimension` sum. Only a space that stores a basis needs a finite one; an algorithm written against exp, log and a metric, or against an inner product, never reads the dimension, which is what lets a space of functions be a space. Held by compile-time checks of the kinds, the sums and the gating of `EuclideanSpace`. Not done: the dimension as a real type parameter of `Vec` (it stays a number there, where it is a count of stored elements).
