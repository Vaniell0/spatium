`algebra/series.hpp`: `Series<T,N>`, a truncated Laurent series in an infinitesimal eps, with its valuation (negative for a pole) and the precision up to which its terms are known kept beside the coefficients. A `Scalar`, so a function written once over `Scalar` with math through ADL runs on it unchanged and what comes out is its behaviour at a point: `limit(f, a)` with x = a + eps, `limit_at_infinity(f)` with x = 1/eps (a point at infinity is a chart; no inf enters an operation), `two_sided_limit`. `Dual` is the case where only the first derivative is wanted. Answered where extrapolation guesses: sin(x)/x, (1 - cos x)/x^2, (sin x - x)/x^3 (three terms cancelled) and (1 + x)^(1/x) at 0, (1 + 1/x)^x and x sin(1/x) at infinity, to 1e-14 and better and, on Real50, to 1e-45; a pole returns as a pole with its order and sign (1/x is +inf on the right and -inf on the left, 1/x^2 is +inf on both; cot x has order -1); two poles that cancel, 1/x - 1/sin x, return the 0 that is left with order 1; the order of vanishing at infinity is read (x/(x^2 + 1) like 1/x, sqrt(x^2 + 1) - x like 1/(2x)), which is what decides an improper integral (the next step: the p-test in `quadrature`). Fractional powers by ramification: sqrt(x) at 0 is a series in t with x = t^2, so `limit` retries x = a + t^r for r in {1, 2, 3, 4, 6} and reports the order as exponent over r (1/sqrt(x) has order -1/2). What a cancellation consumes is recorded in the precision, not hidden. Refused rather than guessed: exp(1/x) (an essential singularity), log x at 0, sin(1/x), sin x at infinity, division by an exact zero -- `Status::NonLaurent` / `Indeterminate`, `LimitKind::Undetermined`. Order is that of eps to 0+ (a < b when the leading term of b - a is positive), so an `if (x < y)` in f means what it should next to a limit from the right. The derivative of a limit passes through with `Dual` as the coefficient (d/dp of lim sin(p x)/x = 1), and `primal_double`/`machine_epsilon` read a Series. Held by closed forms, by the series limit against the function evaluated at a small step (two paths sharing nothing), and by the cases it must refuse. Its first consumers are the p-test in `quadrature` and a pair against the library's own hand-written table: `cos_sinc_of_square`'s six-term series below a threshold and its closed form above it agree with the expansion `Series` derives of cos(sqrt(w)) and sin(sqrt(w))/sqrt(w) (a ramification of 2) to 1e-14 across the threshold, hyperbolic included. That also settles what the kappa family needs: cos(sqrt(w)) and sin(sqrt(w))/sqrt(w) are analytic in w = kappa s through zero and into the negatives, so the bridge at kappa = 0 is the function `cos_sinc_of_square` already is, and `Series` is not the family's engine -- the series serves post-Newtonian expansions in 1/c^2 and asymptotics at infinity. Not done: the kappa family, `Minkowski<N>` in geometric units (c = 1), the inverse trigonometric functions, `acos` near 1.
