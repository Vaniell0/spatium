The user's question, in the middle of a day of merging: had the simplifications of the methods added so far been registered, since each time what was left to do had been lost? The honest answer was partly. Every entry ended in a sentence that began "Not done", but it was prose inside the paragraph, nothing gathered it, and it went stale in one direction and was incomplete in the other: of the 21 entries then in `docs/roadmap/`, the sentences listed `integrate_surface`, a Monte Carlo estimator, the kappa family, `Minkowski<N>` and `riemannian_minimize` without `HasNormal` as still to do long after they were done, and did not mention at all what had been simplified on the way -- the doubly exponential rule's level cap and its reading of a NaN in the interior as the end of a side, Gauss-Kronrod's tolerance halved per half instead of one error budget, the fixed ramifications and term count of a `Series`, Schild's ladder's 1e-8 floor and the pole ladder that is exact and not written, `Method::Auto` being always the same rule, the error of a nested integral being the outer rule's plus the largest inner one. An entry now has a typed `open:` list in its front matter, one item per line, and a later entry says what it did with `resolves: [<date>-<nn>#<k>]`; `scripts/gen_roadmap.py --open` prints what no later entry resolved, grouped by stage, and `--check` (CI) fails on a `resolves` that names nothing. Migrated by hand, not by splitting the old sentences: 65 items were entered across 17 entries, 20 of them were found already resolved by later entries (and say so), and 45 are open -- among them the simplifications above, now each with an id. The list is as honest as the entries are: what no entry mentions is still missing, which is why a simplification now goes into `open:` when it is made.
