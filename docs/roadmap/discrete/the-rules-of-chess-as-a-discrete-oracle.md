The rules of the game live in `discrete/`, and nothing there reads or writes text. `Position::from_setup` decides whether a `PositionSetup` -- what stands on each square, the side to move, castling rights, the en passant square and the two clocks -- is a position the rules can be played from: one king a side, at most sixteen pieces a side, no pawn on the first or last rank, the side that just moved not in check, an en passant square on the rank a double push passes, clocks not negative. `io/chess.hpp` reads and writes FEN, SAN and `e2e4`, and every FEN it reads goes through `from_setup`, so a text that cannot be parsed fails with `ParseError` and one that parses into an unplayable position fails with `from_setup`'s `InvalidArgument`; the tests hold each refused FEN to its code. The starting position built square by square equals the one its FEN reads.

`outcome` ends a game by the rules: mate and stalemate, the fifty-move rule (a mate on the hundredth ply is still a mate), a position dead for want of material, and, given the game, a third repetition counted back to the last capture or pawn move. Two positions repeat when pieces, side, castling rights and possible en passant captures agree: an en passant square no pawn can take on does not make a position new. The test plays 1. e4 Nf6 2. Nf3 Ng8 3. Ng1 Nf6 4. Nf3 Ng8 5. Ng1, which repeats the position after 1. e4 three times; compared as values no earlier position matches (the clocks differ), and compared by the first four fields of the FEN the first is missed and the count is two.

The symmetries of the N-cube, every signed permutation of N axes, are `HyperoctahedralGroup<N>` in `algebra/groups/`, with two actions: on integer vectors and on the cells of a cube of any side. `action.hpp` gives `GroupAction` and `FiniteGroup`, `orbit`, `stabilizer`, `orbits`, and `verify_action`, which checks that the identity moves nothing, that acting by a composite is acting twice, and that |G| = |orbit| |stabiliser| at every sample. A piece of `lattice.hpp` is `algebra::orbit` of its step under the vector action; the 8x8 board's own D4, signed 2x2 matrices on bitboards, is the fast form used by `bitboard.hpp` and `position.hpp`, held to the cell action on all 64 squares. The number of orbits is counted by walking them, by Burnside's lemma (the mean number of fixed points, no orbit looked at), and for a square of side 1 to 9 by the closed form m(m+1)/2 with m = floor((n+1)/2): ten classes on 8x8. A composition that runs the wrong way and an action whose identity moves a cell are each refused, with their reason.

A piece walking at random on the empty board comes back to a cell after 2|E|/deg moves on average, |E| counted in the part of its move graph the cell is in. `test_return_time.cpp` gets that number four ways on every cell for the knight, king, rook and queen: the formula from the degrees, the mean hitting times by `solve_direct` on 64 unknowns, the chain on the ten D4 classes solved the same way times the size of the class, and the sum of n f_n over first-return probabilities. They agree to a relative 1e-10 (the series to 1e-8, at two cells). The knight from a corner takes 168 moves, the king 140, the rook 64 from any cell. The lumped chain exists only because every piece is an orbit of the group: a king with three diagonals taken away cannot be lumped, and the bishop, whose graph is in two parts, takes exactly twice the true time when the formula counts the whole board.

The perft counts of the seven standard positions agree with the published numbers to depth 5 under `[.deep]` (0.76 s, gcc, -O2, on E-cores beside another job). All 49 cases, 2 949 379 assertions, pass with 0 warnings at -Wall -Wextra, and the module units for algebra, discrete and io build.
