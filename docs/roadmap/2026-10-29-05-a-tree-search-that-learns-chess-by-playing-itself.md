`rsc/core/tree_search.hpp` is the PUCT search and the games it plays against itself, knowing nothing of the library: a game is a `SearchGame` (states, moves, the state after a move, how a finished or cut game stands, rows for the moves and for the state, a turn that may be several moves), and the learned parts are an `Evaluator`, a prior over the moves written into the search's buffer and a value in [-1, 1]. It carries forced results up the tree on request (`prove`) and returns the most visited line. Its tests run on Nim, whose answer is exact (a pile is lost for the side to move when it is a multiple of four): with no learning, 600 simulations find the winning take in more than 95% of piles to 12 and one simulation does not; the turn of two moves keeps its sign, and searched as if the sides alternated it would not; and playing itself from a blank start it learns to take the winning stones in more than 90% of piles 5 to 30.

The learned parts are the library's. `algebra/models/network.hpp` is a net of one hidden tanh layer to a number in (-1, 1), templated on the scalar; its backward pass is held against `Dual` carried through the loss, weight by weight, to 1e-12, which needed `tanh` for `Dual`: its derivative is written through the value, 1 - tanh^2, so at 800 it is exactly 1 and flat where sinh / cosh is inf / inf. `Adam` joins AdaGrad in `algebra/analysis/optimize.hpp`: its first step is the rate with the gradient's sign, and scaling every gradient by a thousand moves the steps only through epsilon, where plain gradient descent's scale. `statistics/fit.hpp` is one pass over a sample in a drawn order, the loss and gradient from the caller's objective and the step from a rule; it visits every item once and returns the mean loss before its steps. Fitted by it with Adam, the net learns tanh(2 x0 - x1) to a mean squared error below 0.01 on points it never saw.

`examples/chess_zero` puts them on chess: a softmax over the move rows of `chess_rows.hpp` (13 weights) for the prior, a 13-32-1 tanh net over thirteen numbers of the position (481 weights) for the value, games ended by `discrete::outcome` or cut at 120 plies and decided by three points of material. Its default run, 60 iterations of 24 games at 48 simulations a move on two threads, took 111 s. Against three fixed players, 20 games each, half as each colour, the untrained player scores 0.725 against a random one, 0.075 against a greedy one and 0.025 against a two-ply material search; after the run 0.975, 0.775 and 0.625.
