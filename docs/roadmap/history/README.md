#### Two rules this table exists to enforce

**An item shipped in half says which half in its first line.** Not its third paragraph. This is not style: "no renderer consumes any of it" sat stale for a full day inside an item marked done, and was then quoted back as the current state by the person who wrote it.

**An abandoned item is struck through *with the reason*.** The convention already exists here — see the scattered-items entry under Object model — and simply was not applied everywhere. An item that vanishes silently is indistinguishable from one nobody got to.

Both rules exist because a claim decays in three different places with three different mechanisms, and none of them is visible from the other two: a comment citing a line number rots when the line moves, a ROADMAP sentence rots when the code catches up with it, and a task in an external tracker rots by being invisible. Three scripts in CI catch the first kind. Nothing catches the second, which is what these rules are for.

#### One chain, not four tracks (2026-09-22)

The rows above marked **next** are one line of work, not a menu. RSC is where it starts, physics continues it rather than being a separate subject, and an interactive voxel raytracer is the finale that consumes both. It was written down only outside this repository for two days, which is the failure the index exists to prevent.

The finale has two real claims, and one that must not be made. *Interactive with free flight*, and *the scene is described rather than loaded* — voxels as spaces in the DSL, which makes the raytracer a runtime consumer of the scene format and is the actual reason to build it. Not "the first software ray tracing of Minecraft worlds": Chunky has done CPU path tracing of them since 2010.

The local device decides the shape. Checked 2026-09-24 with `vulkaninfo`: Intel Iris Xe, Mesa, Vulkan 1.4, no `VK_KHR_ray_query`, no `shaderFloat64`. Neither is a loss. A voxel grid is walked by DDA, which is a loop, not a BVH query; and fp32 already passed the black hole's precision gate on all 22 cross-validated cases, worst 3.5e-4 relative at the critical curve against 3.9e-3 for one step of an 8-bit pixel. Where `double` does matter — long integrations whose error accumulates, near-degenerate roots — the work stays on the host.

**The IR is the spine, not a side track (2026-09-24).** The flat field form from #55 has three consumers, and they are the same artifact: the wire format an outside language describes a scene in, what the device evaluates every frame, and what `Dual<T>` differentiates -- which is also how a metric's Christoffel symbols reach a shader with no sympy and no transcription. The outside does not get a copy of the syntax: it sends a description of the `Trace`, C++ builds, optimizes and validates it, and every improvement to `cook()` reaches every language at once. Order: #55; static scene on the device, which already gives free flight since only the camera moves; the GLSL interpreter; the description door; Python; voxels as another leaf kind, with a Minecraft map as a description.

**The black hole's frame rate, and a picture that did not depend on its step until now (2026-09-29).** `blackhole_shader(scene, step_scale)` multiplies the ray's step, and `blackhole_live --step-sweep` renders a reference at a quarter of the tuned step against each multiplier and counts the pixels that differ. Without the disk the geometry -- shadow, rings, stars -- holds to 3x (0.01-0.03% of pixels off by more than 8 levels at 960x540, one hole and the boosted pair) and to 4x within 0.1%. With the disk it did not converge at all: 12% of pixels off even at half the step, because a particle's light was its Gaussian's peak on the chord times min(len, 2.5 sigma), so a particle on the border of two chords counted twice and the picture changed with how the ray was cut. It is now the Gaussian integrated exactly along the chord, exp(-d^2 / 2 sigma^2) sqrt(pi / 2) times two erfs -- the same scale, sqrt(2 pi) for a full crossing -- whose sum over a ray's chords is the integral over the ray however it was cut: 3.4% off at the tuned step, converging. Frame rates at 144p on the Iris Xe (`--bench`, now with fps; `--step`): one hole 42 fps at the tuned step, 118 at 3x; the boosted pair 16 and 39; with 300k particles 21 and 38, 10 and 19 -- past which the particles' move, pack and tree rebuild (17-25 ms at 300k) are half the frame. The step's cap far from the holes is a parameter too (`step_max`, `--step-max`): 5 M instead of 1.5 is another 1.5x at the same picture (the pair without the disk: 699 to 460 ms at 960x540, 0.01% of pixels off), past 5 little is won. The window steps at 3x capped at 5 M -- 0.15% of pixels off, one hole 141 fps, the pair 54 at 144p, where with particles their update is now the larger half; a saved frame and a video keep the tuned step. Both constants are the first a calibration by RSC should own, against the same `--step-sweep` oracle.

**The binary's boost, found by its residual (2026-09-29).** Calibration, RSC's second kind, run on the merger scene's free oracle. `HoleSpec` carries velocity fields beside its path (`binary()` writes both, the Peters shrink included), and `SpacetimeScene::boost(b)` boosts each Kerr-Schild term by b times its hole's velocity -- the event taken to the hole's rest frame, x' = d + k (b.d) b with k = gamma^2 / (gamma + 1) so it stays finite at b = 0, l carried back by the boost's transpose -- all in the metric IR, so the shader gets it unchanged; at b = 0 the pool is what it was. A hole in uniform motion boosted into its frame is exact boosted Kerr, and the test holds its residual to 1e-12 against 1e-4 unboosted. Then `rsc/tools/metric_calibrate.cpp` picks b by minimising the exact residual (over the local tidal scale, median over events 3-8 M from each hole at random orbital times), by golden section, and reports the midpoint beside it. Where physics knows the answer the method finds it: for equal masses b* is 0.79 at 10 M, 0.85 at 14, 0.91 at 20, 0.93 at 40 -- towards 1, the boost at each hole's own velocity, as the superposition becomes exact with distance; 1.10 and 1.04 at 1:3. Where it does not, it finds a correction: close in, less than the full boost is better, the residual compensating the superposition's nonlinearity. The residual by the holes falls 2.4-5x (0.73 to 0.30 at 10 M, 0.33 to 0.063 at 40 M), between them 1.4-2x. On the device the boosted pool is 255 ops against 137 and the trace 16.9 ms against 12.9 at 144p (`blackhole_live --check`, which now checks the boosted pair too), with device against double still 7e-6; `blackhole_live` boosts its pair, the library's default stays 0.

**From RSC to CCD: a search base, not a dispatcher (2026-09-25).** The reason RSC comes before CCD is that complex scenes should get *cheaper* than the usual barrier-plus-Newton, and that needs search integrated into the rest of the code. Continuous collision is a search for the first contact in time. Over one step a body's motion is a curve on its configuration manifold -- a point in R^3, a rigid body in SE(3) with the screw motion as `exp_map`, a body confined to a surface on the surface itself -- so the curve is known exactly, and distance to an obstacle is 1-Lipschitz in that manifold's metric: advancing by a lower bound on it is safe, and tighter than a Euclidean bound driven by a rotating body's fastest point. With time as a coordinate the query is a ray in configuration x time, and the manifold tracing machinery serves both rendering and collision. RSC sits on top: its value head decides which interval of time to refine and when a certificate already settles it, while correctness is held by the certificate -- a lower bound proving an interval empty -- and never by the network. Order: correctness of the existing sweeps to the quality bar first, then the geodesic sweep on a configuration manifold, then the search.

**Stage 0 done: the sweeps step by a floor (2026-09-25).** `sweep_point_surface` stepped by `point_to`, the distance to the point a local search found -- an upper bound -- and flew through a thin spike between the seeds of that search; it also reported contact at time zero with a tube three units away, because it read "inside" off the chart's normal orientation. Both were run against the old code and seen to fail. Now `distance_bound` (narrow_phase.hpp) gives a certified lower bound -- exact for the sphere and torus, `|f|/L` for an implicit surface, branch and bound for a chart given its Lipschitz constant, with an optional per-cell reach for charts that degenerate -- and `sweep_sphere_surface` advances by it, with a relative contact tolerance, a ball radius, a budget of evaluations and a `certified` flag. A bare `Surface` has no certified path: without a Lipschitz constant no finite sample proves a miss. A fuzz against the closed forms (quadric, exact torus distance), half of it grazing, finds no late answer and no miss. What it costs is the input to the search stage: the spike takes 13.7M evaluations under one global constant; a graze ends early, by up to 1% of the step at 64 iterations, because advancement crawls as x^2 along a tangent; a ball against a chart needs cells as 1/gap, so its answer is early by 1/budget. The two audit items on grazing roots -- lost near-real quadric roots, the unnormalised ray in `sweep_sphere_sphere` -- did not reproduce from 1e-6 to 1e6 and were left alone.

**The search base, first form (2026-09-25).** A chart is now searched over cells of parameters x time, earliest time first, after a few moves of advancement: a cell whose floor is above the tolerance is dropped, and when the earliest cell left shows a real contact at its centre its start is a certified time of impact (`first_contact`, `ChartCellTree` in rigid_contact.hpp). The cells -- a `Ball` each, from `spatial/bound.hpp` -- are kept across queries, so a second pass over the same surface evaluates nothing. Against advancement alone, on the same fuzz and at a like cost: sphere grazes at most 8e-4 of the step early instead of 9.6e-3; torus grazes early on 98 of 617 instead of 493; the spike 842k evaluations instead of 13.3M. No late answer and no miss at any budget. What it leaves is the first-order limit: a flat minimum -- a ball against a chart, a path that passes a surface by a hair before it touches it -- still costs cells as 1/gap, and that is the input to a bound of second order and to the learned ordering, not to more budget.

**CCD as chains of primitives, and how little of the chain space is there (2026-09-26).** `rsc/include/ccd_ops.hpp` cuts continuous collision into four primitives over one state -- the closed form, k moves of advancement, the search with a budget of cells, a speculative jump over a slice of the step -- and `rsc/tools/ccd_synthesize.cpp` enumerates every chain of up to three of them (819) per class of query, an obstacle and a way of moving past it, against an exact truth. A chain is right on a class when it is never late, never misses, never reports a contact that is not there and is at most 1e-3 of the step early. Queries of a class share one cell tree, as a solver's vertices do against one obstacle in a step, and the cost counts the cells walked as well as the ones made: counting only new cells had priced a second pass over a split tree at zero. At 20 queries a class: where a closed form exists it decides at a cost of 1 and nothing is left to search -- that is read off the object, not found. On charts the best chain beats the hybrid by 12-14x head-on and through on the sphere, 1.1-2.1x on the torus and at grazes, where the cost is walking a deep tree and no setting of the four knobs changes it; a torus graze is answered right on every query only by A32 S20 at 3.7M a query, 15x the hybrid, which is wrong on 2 of 20. **The space collapses.** Fingerprinting what each chain answers, the 819 chains are 22-109 distinct algorithms per class (answers bit for bit), 42-227 counting cost too, 10-109 with times rounded to 1e-9 -- an effective branching of 2.5-4.4 out of 9 by answers, between the integer chains' 2.13 of 5 and the meshes' 4.66 of 6. The reason is the state: one number, how far the step is proved clear, so A4 A4 is A8 and two short jumps are one long one. What this sends the search to is primitives that do not commute over a state that keeps intermediate results -- a frontier of (cell x time) pieces, split in space or in time, cut by a ball, a Lipschitz bound or a cone of normals -- with a policy over the split and a value head only where breadth-first runs out of budget, as `rsc/README.md` orders it for synthesis.

**A floor of second order: a cell between two planes (2026-09-26).** The first-order limit the search base left -- a flat minimum costs cells as 1/gap -- is gone where a chart bounds its second derivatives (`LipschitzChart::second_uu`/`second_vv`, or `cell_second` per cell). Along any unit direction the height of a cell's image differs from the bilinear interpolant of its corners by at most (du^2 |f_uu| + dv^2 |f_vv|) / 8, so the cell lies in a slab whose thickness is second order in its size; the direction only decides the thickness, never the validity, so it is taken across the cell's diagonals and needs no derivative. The slab's floor drifts only with the motion across it, and a graze barely moves across it. On 244 torus grazes at 2^14: none more than 1e-3 early against 32, the worst 1.3e-8 of the step against 0.45, at 32k evaluations and cells walked a graze against 53k. In the chain synthesis at 20 queries a class the torus graze that took A32 S20 at 3.7M a query takes P10 P10 S12 at 14k, and the sphere's graze through its chart 13k instead of 270k; the hybrid is now right on every torus graze instead of 18 of 20. Where the slab does not help -- head-on -- its corners cost: the sphere's best chart chain went from 582 to 858. Two things this got wrong on the way, both measured: a rule that split time whenever the slab asked split it forever head-on, since the slab thins as the square of a cell while the motion across it does not; and a bound on |f_uu| over the whole domain made a sphere's cells near the poles thick -- r sin v is nearly zero there -- and ended two grazes 0.074 early that first order had brought within 2.3e-4. And one about memory: a class's cell tree shared across twenty queries at 2^20 cells each reached 12 GB in 29 s, so a kept tree is trimmed at 2^20 cells after each query, as a solver's would be.

**Two moving surfaces, against the published state of the art (2026-09-26).** `physics/mechanics/surface_ccd.hpp` finds the first contact between two surfaces whose points move linearly over the step -- a Bezier patch with moving control points, or any chart paired with a chart of velocities -- by searching pairs of cells x time with the ball and the slab of the one-surface search. What is new against that search is that time is never split while the cells are coarse: with the cells fixed, both floors are functions of t alone -- the ball a quadratic, the slab the minimum of sixteen lines from the corners, which move linearly -- so each pair's interval is cut exactly to where contact is still possible. Compared on the random test of Chen et al.'s time-dependent inclusion method (SIGGRAPH Asia 2024, the fastest variant, OBBs), same generator and seed, same compiler flags, one thread, 1000 cases (`benchmarks/external/tdib_compare.cpp`): cubic patches 28.4 ms a query against 2.67 at a width of 1e-5, 47.0 against 2.97 at 1e-6, 15.9 against 2.35 at 1e-4 -- ours grows with the precision asked about as its logarithm; quadratic patches 15.5 against 0.89, bilinear 3.9 against 0.19. The same cases collide for both (987, 921, 704 of 1000), and no answer of either is later than a Newton witness -- a time at which a point of one surface is found on the other -- found for every contact. What ours needs of a surface is bounds on its first and second derivatives, not control points, so a torus from its formula goes through the same code; what it does not do yet is Chen et al.'s bunny-and-torus scene, whose rational patches need derivative bounds of their own.

**Vertex-face and edge-edge against Tight Inclusion, and a policy per kind of query (2026-09-26).** The same search takes a vertex as a chart of no extent, an edge as a chart in one parameter and a triangle as a bilinear chart collapsed at a corner, so the queries of cloth and meshes are its special cases rather than a second code path. On the Sample-Queries set of Wang et al.'s benchmark (TOG 2021), 207 279 queries with a ground truth, against Tight Inclusion's own `vertexFaceCCD`/`edgeEdgeCCD` at the benchmark's settings (`benchmarks/external/ti_compare.cpp`): vertex-face 1.41 us a query for both, edge-edge 0.33 us against 7.65; no false negative for either, false positives 111 against 85 and 143 against 137. Edges have no normal, so they are apart only along the cross product of their directions, which the search now tries (`SurfaceCcdPolicy::kTangentCross`); among pairs that start at one moment the smaller is taken first. The directions tried and the side halved are now a `SurfaceCcdPolicy`, since no correctness depends on them, and the best of them differs by the kind of query -- on the patch test, bilinear patches are fastest with normals and cross products (55x TDIB-CCD), quadratic with all three (22x), cubic with normals and the centre line (11.4x), while normals alone make cubic patches twice as slow as TDIB-CCD. The default, all three, is within 20% of the best on each; choosing per kind of query is the job for RSC.

**RSC in the CCD pipeline: a chooser, and a finding kept as code (2026-09-26).** `core/concepts.hpp` gains `Chooser<C, F>` -- `c.choose(f)` -- the seam where a learned choice enters an algorithm that does not know it; any chooser is valid, since a setting moves cost and never correctness. `surface_ccd.hpp` computes `SurfaceCcdFeatures` from the charts alone (parameters each side has, second derivatives over first, relative speed over size) and takes a chooser in place of a policy. `rsc/tools/ccd_policy.cpp` runs every query of six kinds (`rsc/include/ccd_queries.hpp`: Bezier patches of order 1-3, vertex-triangle, edge-edge, a torus against a patch) under all 32 policies, costs each as pairs plus slab directions -- deterministic, so labels do not move with load -- and fits a cost-sensitive tree whose leaves take the least total cost relative to each query's cheapest; the first fit, on absolute costs, spent every split on patches and made vertex-face 2.15x slower. The tree is written as a header, `rsc/include/generated/ccd_chooser_tree.hpp`, with the held-out table it was chosen on, and `tests/test_rsc_ccd.cpp` holds it there: same answers as the default, and no more cost on queries from a seed it never saw; when the primitives change and the finding goes stale, that test says so. Held out, time against the default: torus-patch 0.80, cubic 0.93, edge-edge 0.94, vertex-face 0.96, bilinear 0.97, quadratic 1.00; the per-query best would be 0.66-0.95 in cost. So over these four slab directions and two split rules a choice per query is worth 5-20%: the large factors came from the floors, and a choice with more to win needs finer primitives to choose between.

**The connectivity matrix (2026-09-29).** Consistency checked by a machine rather than remembered: `scripts/gen_connectivity.py` builds every (scalar x space x probe) cell of `tests/connectivity/probes.hpp` in its own translation unit -- 6 scalars (double, float, long double, Real50, Dual, Dual²), 8 spaces (E3, S2, H2, S2xE1, SPD log-Euclidean and affine-invariant, a torus chart, a sphere level set), 7 probes (metric axioms, closed-form distance against |log|_g, exp after log, midpoint, Fréchet mean, the library's `verify_exp_log`, a derivative through Dual against a difference quotient) -- and grades it: L0 compiles, L1 finite, L2 its axiom or symmetry holds, L3 its numbers agree with the same cell over the reference scalar (double against Real50, the rest against double). `docs/connectivity.md` is the table, `tests/connectivity/expected.hpp` the levels `test_connectivity.cpp` holds 230 compiling cells to, and CI regenerates both and fails on a difference. First run: 225 of 336 at L2 or above. Every remaining red cell is the library's, not a probe's: `ParametricSurface`/`ImplicitSurface` `log_map` does not compile (the audit's item 8, 60 cells); `Dual` lacks `abs`, `log`, `cosh`, `acosh`, `fmod`; `Real50` fails in `solve_quadratic` and `fmod`; and a new bug, `Sphere::exp_map` dropping the derivative at t = 0 by a branch on the value of a Dual. Getting there, the matrix caught four mistakes in its own probes first -- points made in double not lying on the sphere in Real50, a difference quotient taken after rounding to double, tangents used at the wrong point -- each by a cell disagreeing with its neighbour over another scalar.

**The matrix fills its own gaps (2026-09-29).** Two axes that ask symmetry questions rather than single ones: products of spaces the matrix finds green one by one (S2xH2, H2xE3, S2xS2, SPD log-Euclidean x S2, SPD affine-invariant x S2), which must be green together, and a cylinder that is a space by ADL alone -- no members, types through `space_traits` -- templated over every scalar. 630 cells, 453 at L2 or above. Where the prediction held, nothing to report; where it did not, three findings. `core/access.hpp`'s `point_t` read `space_traits<const S>` and missed the specialisation for `S` (fixed: through `remove_cvref`). `ProductSpace::project` needed both factors to be Surfaces, so SPD x S2 had none and its points could not be put back on the sphere over Real50 -- green factors, red product (fixed: each part by its own project, a part without one left as it is). And two gaps kept open: `ProductSpace` takes only `Vec` points and member-based factors, so neither the matrix-valued SPD nor the ADL cylinder can be a factor; the library's verifiers ask for members too. `Dual` gains `trunc`, which its new `fmod` needs one level down.

**Products and verifiers on the customization points (2026-09-29).** The first of the plan's second stage, predicted by the matrix and checked by it. `ProductSpace` asks nothing of its factors but what `core/access.hpp` can reach -- a factor's distance, exp, log, metric and projection by member, by ADL or derived -- and its points are one `Vec` when both factors' are Vecs, as before, or a `Pair` with the vector arithmetic tangents need when a factor's are not (SPD's affine-invariant matrices). `verify_metric` and `verify_exp_log` go the same way, so the library can check a space it did not write. The matrix, regenerated: 453 to 526 of 630 cells at L2 or above -- SPD affine-invariant x S2 and the ADL cylinder x E3 green over five scalars of six, the cylinder's `verify_exp_log` green -- and nothing that was green went red. On the way, the Vec test first took a Matrix for a Vec (both keep their entries in `data`); it now asks for a specialisation of `Vec`.

**A space from its metric alone (2026-10-02).** The second item of the plan's second stage: `spaces/metric_chart.hpp`. `MetricChart<T, N, Metric>` takes a metric -- a callable on any scalar, `Vec<S,N> -> Matrix<S,N,N>` -- and derives the rest: Christoffel symbols from `Dual` partials of g, exp by the geodesic flow, log by Newton shooting on exp, distance and midpoint by the customization points. Three pieces had to be written for it. `integrate_extrapolated` (Gragg-Bulirsch-Stoer, `algebra/ode.hpp`) takes a tolerance as a power of the scalar's own epsilon, so one call reaches 4e-11 on double and 2e-33 on `Real50`, where a fixed RK4 step could not at any practical count; `christoffel`, `geodesic_rhs` and `geodesic_step` went from four dimensions to N, and `physics/relativity/geodesic.hpp` now names them (bit-identical to the old path on 1280 values of Kerr and Schwarzschild; where g does not invert it returns NaN, where it used to read the symbols as zero and fly on straight); `machine_epsilon<T>()` and `primal_double` read through Dual layers. Held against closed forms in `tests/test_metric_chart.cpp`: the sphere in (theta, phi) is `Sphere<2>`, the upper half-plane's vertical geodesics and distance are the closed ones. The matrix grew by three spaces -- the sphere and the half-plane derived, and the half-plane derived times `Sphere<2>`, a derived factor beside a closed-form one -- 756 cells, 660 at L2 or above, and all three are L3 over all six scalars. Four defects found by the cells, not by looking: `Dual<Real50>` had no three-way comparison (Boost numbers have none; `<=>` now goes through `<` and `==`); `Dual<Dual<Dual<T>>>` was not a `Scalar` (`T{0}` needs three user conversions in a row; a numeric constructor reaches any depth); the zero-length interval returned its start and dropped the derivative, so d/dt of the flow at t = 0 read 0 where it is the velocity (found by the derivative cell, AD against a difference quotient); and a Real50 Frechet-mean cell on the derived sphere ran past ten minutes, because the probe's samples lay 1.3 rad out where the Karcher iteration contracts by 0.64 a step -- the samples now lie half as far. Cells that take minutes over fifty digits (the derived spaces over `Real50`) are not run by `test_connectivity`; the CI job that regenerates the matrix holds them.

**A chart keeps its callables' types (2026-09-28).** `MovingChart<T, P, W>` holds its position and velocity as the callables themselves, `std::function` only by default, and `moving_chart()` builds one that keeps them; the search, its cell tree and its features are templated on them. This is the first of the September audit's "one laziness model" steps taken where it was cheapest -- the CCD this month was written with erased charts, and the napkins scene had already worked around the heap allocation a lambda holding its points costs behind a 16-byte `std::function`. On Sample-Queries, same answers (no false negative, the same false positives): vertex-face 1.685 to 1.575 us a query, edge-edge 0.374 to 0.317 -- 7 and 15%; what is left is the search's own allocations, a cell vector and a queue per query.

**One search for a point and for a surface (2026-09-26).** A `MovingChart` now carries a thickness -- contact is the thicknesses' sum, a ball's radius or a sheet's half-thickness -- and per-cell position bounds (`cell_bp`), so the one-surface query of rigid_contact.hpp is a two-surface query with one side a point. The two paths against the quartic's first root on 394 torus grazes: a point, 1.7 ms a query through `first_contact`'s cell tree and 0.06 ms through surface_ccd, neither ever late; a ball of radius 0.05, 6.7 ms and 74 answers more than 1e-3 early against 155 ms and none. The ball is the flat minimum both paths pay for, and what removes it is not a better search but a different problem -- the centre against the surface pushed out by the radius, where the minimum is sharp again: for a torus that is a torus, for a chart it needs bounds on its curvature. Meanwhile surface_ccd brackets its answer: a pair whose centres are witnessed within the thicknesses is an upper bound, the earliest open start a lower one, and the search stops when they meet (`SurfaceCcdPolicy::witness_stop`) -- 236 to 155 ms on the ball, nothing moved on the patch and Sample-Queries comparisons (never later than a witness; no false negative; edge-edge false positives 146 against 143). A torus falling flat onto a plane touches along a whole circle at once, the same flat minimum along a curve: at a width of 1e-6 it took the full 2^24 pairs without the bracket and 4.5M with it. The bracket's witness is weak there -- two cells' centres within the tolerance, which needs both cells tiny -- where a centre within the tolerance of the other cell's slab, a plane for a flat cell, would witness at once.

**Napkins: cloth against cloth, and the benchmark it makes (2026-09-26).** `examples/napkin_scene.hpp` drops or throws several XPBD napkins onto a sphere, a floor and each other; `examples/napkins` measures a substep by its parts and counts, independently of every method in it, the edges of the cloth that pierce a triangle of it -- by a segment-triangle test after each substep. The first version put cloth-against-cloth contact after the solver, as a push and then a continuous pass, and it failed in a way worth recording: a stack at rest was pulled through itself by the solver every substep, the continuous pass could not separate it in four rounds and reverted the whole substep, and from 1.5 s on the cloth hung frozen in the air with zero piercings that meant nothing. Contact is now a constraint inside the solver's iterations -- every vertex-triangle and edge-edge pair near enough, held `thickness` apart on the side it started -- and surface_ccd.hpp's queries follow as the guarantee, behind the standard coplanarity filter (the signed volume's cubic, whose Bernstein coefficients of one sign prove no crossing; 84% of pairs never reach the solver). Dropped from 0.75 m at 240 substeps a second, the constraints alone hold and the continuous pass never stops a pair. Thrown at 8 m/s at 60 substeps a second, one a frame: 4 napkins of 13x13 pierce 3 545 times without the continuous pass and never with it, at 9 ms a substep on one thread; 8 of 13x13, 311 586 against 0 at 20 ms; 8 of 19x19, 28 560 against 0 at 47 ms, one substep of 90 reverted whole. The layout itself was checked the same way and was found crossing before the first step, 7 piercings from neighbouring napkins tilted towards each other; the measure now refuses to start from a layout that already crosses.

**One black hole program, described rather than coded (2026-09-26).** `blackhole_demo.cpp` and `blackhole_gr_demo.cpp` each hard-coded a metric, a disk and a camera path in ~1500 lines between them; both are removed. `examples/blackhole_live.cpp` renders a `SpacetimeScene` -- one hole or a pair on a quadrupole inspiral, the metric Minkowski plus a Kerr-Schild term per hole, written as IR fields and lowered to GLSL with exact derivatives -- on the Iris Xe in a window (144p at 60 fps by default), to a frame, or to a frame sequence on any Vulkan device. What holds it to physics is measured, not asserted: the device against the host in float and double for rays and for dust, a Schwarzschild shadow within a third of a pixel of 3 sqrt(3) M / D sqrt(1 - 2M/D), and each metric's vacuum residual. `gpu/`'s CUDA kernels stay, as the transcription of the removed demo they were checked against.

**The disk is its matter, drawn through a tree (2026-09-26).** The disk was a procedural density with dust laid over it in a 256x256x32 grid -- two substances, and the grid blurred the dust to fog over the photon rings. Now the disk is one substance: test particles on the scene's geodesics, seeded as clumps already sheared by Keplerian rotation, so they draw thin spiral streams; each is packed as a sphere instance and `DeviceLbvh` builds a tree over them on the device every frame, which each RK4 chord of a ray walks. The Doppler shift is each particle's own, from its 4-velocity. What a frame costs on the Iris Xe (`blackhole_live --bench`, median of five):

| scene | particles | 144p: matter (move+pack / boxes / sort / nodes) | 144p trace | 540p trace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one Kerr hole | 0 | -- | 22 ms | 352 ms |
| one Kerr hole | 300k | 5.3 / 2.0 / 5.4 / 3.6 ms | 22 ms | 386 ms |
| one Kerr hole | 1M | 18 / 6.7 / 12 / 13 ms | 25 ms | 400 ms |
| a pair | 300k | 7.3 / 2.0 / 3.9 / 3.6 ms | 39 ms | 601 ms |
| a pair | 1M | 24 / 6.5 / 11 / 13 ms | 50 ms | 642 ms |

The trace dominates and hardly notices the matter, since a ray's chord that misses the root box asks nothing; the matter's own cost is linear in the particles, the host's sort a third of it. The quality pass before this (a finer step near the photon orbit, a lower transmittance floor, for the rings) made the trace about a third dearer: 960x540 went from 0.26 s to 0.35 s.

Newton's cradle belongs in the middle rather than beside it. It is the canonical case where a naive integrator *visibly* fails, so it is the first demo in the tree that shows an invariant rather than a motion.
