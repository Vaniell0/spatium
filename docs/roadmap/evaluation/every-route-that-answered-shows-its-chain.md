Only the route taken wrote its steps, so a quadratic solved in closed form showed the discriminant's chain and nothing of the second route that proved its roots or of the check by Vieta's formulas. The sheet now carries `routes`, one entry per candidate that answered, in the order they were tried, each with its cost, whether it was taken, a gist and its own chain: the closed form's, Vieta's formulas (the equation, its coefficients, the sum and the product of the roots), and every root proved -- the box searched, how many pieces it was cut into and how many hold no root (`Roots::pieces`, `Roots::excluded`, counted by `roots_on`, and the same for a system), then each root as Newton's or Krawczyk's step proved it. A number writes its computation, a linear system its elimination, an inequality its ends and the set. A nonlinear system's roots show each one's box, and each equation on that box is checked to hold zero; its undecided pieces are listed, eight and then how many more. A piece undecided past 10^300 says it is where the equation overflows a double. The gallery keeps the routes without their costs, as it does the candidates. The check: the quadratic's routes are the three named, one taken, its chain the sheet's Steps; seven more questions each give routes with a chain and at most one taken; the system's two roots each hold zero on their box (`test_rsc_respond`); with no root every piece is excluded, with roots some are not (`test_interval`). A last section, Next, says what else a root alone on its page can become, each a question written in the language with its LaTeX: an equation where it is greater, on an interval about its roots, and its integral between the outer roots; an inequality where it is equal, and where it does not hold. Each offered question is one the library answers (`test_rsc_respond` asks every one).
