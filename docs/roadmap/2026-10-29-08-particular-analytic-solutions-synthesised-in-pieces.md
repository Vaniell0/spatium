`rsc/spatium/synthesis.hpp` finds a particular analytic solution of a job on an interval: the reference, the tolerance, the operations allowed and how large an expression may grow are the job, and they are the set the search runs over. A candidate is a field of the scene IR in one variable, so what is found is evaluated by the IR's interpreter and lowered to plain data with nothing written for it; the test holds the lowered form to the field at 200 points, bit for bit. Every expression of the operations up to five nodes over the interval's own variable t = (x - centre) * 2 / width and over x is built from the smaller ones, and one whose values at 48 sample points equal an earlier one's is dropped -- the collapse that keeps it finite (463 distinct expressions for + - * / sqrt sin). The answer is a sum of up to four of them with coefficients fitted by least squares through modified Gram-Schmidt: all sets of one and two terms are tried, larger ones grow greedily from the best pair with each term swapped for any other while that lowers the error, and it is accepted when the largest error on 2001 Chebyshev points is within the tolerance. 3x^2 + 2 sin x on [0.1, 1] comes back as two terms, coefficients 3 and 2, error 1.6e-15.

Where nothing is accepted the interval is halved, down to a narrowest width. log(1 + x) on [0, 1], with no logarithm among the operations, takes 1, 4, 16 and 76 pieces at tolerances 1e-3, 1e-5, 1e-7 and 1e-9. Three things changed the counts on the way: the interval's own variable (on a narrow interval powers of x are all but constant, and at 1e-6 the deflection took 727 pieces with 105 unaccepted before it and 69 with one after), least squares by Gram-Schmidt in place of the normal equations, and trying every pair before growing greedily (the greedy pick alone missed 3x^2 + 2 sin x, leaving 1.2e-2).

The first job is the deflection of a light ray of impact parameter b by a Schwarzschild hole, in x = 1/b from b = 10^4 to b_c (1 + 1e-4), b_c = 3 sqrt 3. Its reference is held two ways: the integral by the library's quadrature with u = u0 - s^2 taking the root at the end away (without it the quadrature is off by 3e-8 and says so, not trusted) against Darwin's closed form through std::comp_ellint_1 and std::ellint_1, to 1e-12 or better from b = b_c (1 + 1e-4) to 1000. At 1% it is 11 pieces, the first from b = 20.8 to 10^4, more than half below b = 6; at 1e-4 19 pieces; at 1e-6 59, the last, next to b_c, not reached at the narrowest width. Near b_c every decade nearer costs pieces at the same tolerance, and an end 2e-8 from the divergence in x is not reached with pieces no narrower than 1e-5.
