Stage 2.7. `spaces/integrate_volume.hpp`: `integrate_volume(g, f, domains...)` is the integral of f(x) sqrt|det g(x)| over a product of one-dimensional domains -- finite, half line or whole line -- and `volume_of(g, domains...)` is f = 1: the area of a surface, the length of a curve, the volume of a space given by a metric and nothing else. It is the door of `integrate` applied once per coordinate (Fubini, innermost last), with sqrt|det g|, the absolute value being what covers a pseudo-Riemannian metric and, for the Lorentzian signature, -det g. Held by closed forms that come from nowhere near the code: the area of a torus, 4 pi^2 R r, to 1e-10 relative; the whole unit sphere through the stereographic chart, two infinite domains at once with nothing sampled at the pole the chart leaves out, 4 pi to twelve digits (value 12.566370614359, estimate 6e-13), and its moment of z^2, 4 pi/3; the area of a hyperbolic disk in geodesic polar coordinates, 2 pi (cosh rho - 1), at three radii; the length of a circle; and a metric under which the plane has infinite area, which is not called converged. The status is weighed, not carried up: at the farthest nodes of an infinite outer rule (x of order 1e39 for the sphere's chart) the inner integral is 1e-117 and its rule honestly says it ran out of levels, which is immaterial to a total of 12.57 and had made the whole answer `DepthCapped`; so a failure or a divergence at any level always counts, and a rule that merely did not converge counts only when its own error is larger than the outer tolerance would let through. The error estimate is the outer rule's plus the largest estimate of any inner integral -- an estimate, as everywhere. Found on the way: a NaN in the integrand inside the domain (not at a far node) is read by the doubly exponential rule as the end of that side, so `integrate` of a function that is NaN on half of [0, 1] returns 0.5008 with status `DepthCapped` where `Failed` with NaN is the honest answer (not fixed here; the status already does not say `Converged`). Not done: a metric whose partials are finite differences (an erased surface) does not go through this at all -- `pullback_metric` takes a map that is Dual-evaluable -- so the volume it gives is exact in its partials by construction, and what an erased surface's area costs in honesty is not yet said by its type.
